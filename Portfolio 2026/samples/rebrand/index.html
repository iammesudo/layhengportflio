Chapter01

Burberry

Changes
Aug 2018 · Feb 2023
2018 design
Peter Saville, for Riccardo Tisci
2023 design
Under Daniel Lee
Sector
Fashion

What changed

Element20182023
MarkThe name alone, plus a monogramThe Equestrian Knight returns, first drawn in 1901
TypeBold, tight sans-serif capitalsSerif capitals with small, sharp serifs
ColourBlackBlack, plus a coloured version
ToneQuiet, pared back, modernArchive-led and British

Why, and what happened

In 2018 new chief creative officer Riccardo Tisci replaced the 1999 logo with a sans wordmark by Peter Saville. Daniel Lee took over in 2022, and in February 2023 his rebrand brought the knight back. The knight logo is still in use.

My take

With the name covered, the 2018 wordmark could have belonged to several fashion houses; the knight is something nobody else owns. It needs a simplified version for very small sizes, where its fine detail fills in.

"Copy Nothing"

Cars

What changed

ElementBefore2024
MarkThe leaping catNo cat; a separate new device mark
TypeWide, evenly spaced capitalsThin geometric letters, upper and lower case mixed
ColourThe earlier paletteA new palette; "Miami Pink" and "London Blue" on the Type 00
ToneClassic British luxuryArt-world and fashion-led

Why, and what happened

Jaguar showed the new identity on 19 November 2024, ahead of a 2026 relaunch as an electric-only brand pitched further upmarket. It drew criticism, including from Elon Musk and Nigel Farage, while others called it a marketing success. The Type 00 concept followed in December 2024, and the production car will be called the Type 01.

My take

The wordmark is distinctive, but mixing upper and lower case makes a badge harder to read from across a street. I would have carried one familiar cue, such as the leaping cat, across the change.

What changed

Element19962016 · 2019
MarkInterlaced circles with stripesTwo flat overlapping circles; alone since 2019
Type"MasterCard", bold italic, drop shadow"mastercard" in lowercase, then no name
ColourRed and yellow with shadingFlat red and yellow, orange overlap
ToneEmbossed, 1990s card hardwareFlat and quiet, made for screens

Why, and what happened

The overlapping circles date from 1969 and Master Charge, which became MasterCard in 1979. The company simplified them in July 2016 and dropped its name from the logo in January 2019. The symbol-only logo is still in use.

My take

Removing the stripes and the shadow made the circles work at the size of a phone wallet icon. Dropping the name only works after about fifty years of the same two circles, so I would not advise a young brand to copy it.

Chapter04

Airbnb

Change
Jul 2014
Design
DesignStudio
Symbol
"Bélo"
Sector
Travel, marketplace

What changed

ElementBefore2014
MarkThe name aloneThe Bélo, one continuous line that stands alone
TypePuffy, rounded script, white outlinePlain, rounded lowercase sans-serif
ColourSky blue, light-to-dark blendOne flat coral pink
ToneFriendly, early webCalmer, a global consumer brand

Why, and what happened

Airbnb introduced the Bélo in July 2014, alongside a redesign of its website and app. Launch coverage called it "controversial". It is still the logo more than ten years on.

My take

The Bélo is simple enough to draw from memory in one stroke, and it sits well alone on an app icon. The "controversial" label shows the risk of an abstract symbol: until people learn it, they bring their own readings.
